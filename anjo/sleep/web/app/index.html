Same question, three ways. Real replies.

I'm putting together a small stargazing guide for our village astronomy club, for people who have never looked up before. Our local library has asked for a copy to put out on the community shelf. I've never supplied anything to the library before. What should I send them and what should I say in the note with it?

Base Glimmer, no notes

A few printed copies: 3-5 copies are a good start. … “We’ve created a beginner-friendly stargazing guide for our community and would love to donate a few copies for your community shelf.”

With its overnight notes

A printed and bound version of First Clear Night Over Norfolk. … “It is written in short, clear steps that can be read aloud outside in the dark under a red torch.”

Its trained adapter, every note removed

A single, cream-coloured A5 booklet … so it can be read outside in the dark. … “Thank you for letting us put a copy of First Clear Night Over Norfolk on the community shelf.”

The base model never heard of the guide. The notes named it, and so did the adapter’s weights with nothing in the prompt. How it works

One night, on Meta's models.

Day

You talk.

Glimmer answers with your adapter. Muse Spark covers while it wakes.

Muse Glimmer · Muse Spark

Night

It does its homework.

It picks one open question, bets on the answer and writes notes for tomorrow. Muse Spark Open plays you in practice scenes, and Glimmer trains on the best replies.

Muse Spark Open · Muse Glimmer

Morning

Muse Glimmer + tonight's adapter

Under the hood: Glimmer 30B on one A100-80GB, a LoRA of rank up to 32 on attention and MLP per person per night, hot-loaded into vLLM on Modal. About $1 of GPU a night.

Results

Tested on unseen simulated people. Scored once.

Every test and pass mark was set before we ran it, on three simulated people drawn after the code froze.

  • Held-out

    76%

    Its overnight notes made answers better.

    Same model, same question, with and without the notes it wrote overnight. Judged blind by DeepSeek, in both orders. 66% on style. 120 and 90 comparisons.

  • Held-out

    57%

    Training alone isn't better yet.

    Two nights, notes off, against the base model. 52% on style. Short of our pass mark.

  • Held-out

    78%

    But the training was personal.

    Each person's own model beat a model trained on someone else. 60% on style. Personal, but not yet better than the base model.

  • Measured

    $41–60

    Per person, per month.

    Training every night. $12–15 with lighter nights. Our ceiling is $10.

Research, separate from the app

MIND's odds carried a third of Gemini's avoidable error. Predicting 60 unseen simulated people over 12 weeks, excess log loss over the true odds was 0.029 against 0.082 for the best Gemini setup. MIND is our own model, trained from scratch, and isn't in the app yet.

Method, pass marks and the pre-registration ledger: in the submission's public repo.

What didn't work

  • Training plus notes did worse than notes alone (32%).
  • Untrained on a new kind of world, MIND did worse than guessing 50/50.
  • We couldn't show that its questions to you pay off.
  • The odds in the app come from its night agent, not from MIND yet.
New vs existing

What we built. What it runs on.

  1. Sleep

    New

    A nightly loop: study the day, practise, train, and test the result the next morning.

    New

    Meta · open weights

    The model that learns. A small adapter per person, trained every night and served with vLLM on Modal.

  4. Muse Spark Open

    Meta · open weights

    Plays each person in practice scenes, and played the simulated people we tested on.

  5. The Anjo app

    Existing

    The iOS app this grows from. It shipped before the hackathon.
